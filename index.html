E-Commerce Sales Analysis

Revenue, order trends and regional performance in the Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset, about 100,000 orders from 2016 to 2018.

What I did

  • Loaded 7 tables into MySQL and joined orders, items and customers.
  • Used delivered orders only, with revenue based on item price and excluding freight.
  • Wrote four SQL queries, then rebuilt the same numbers as Power BI measures to confirm they match.

Findings

  • São Paulo is the largest market at about R$ 5.07M. SP, RJ and MG together bring in about 63% of revenue.
  • Revenue grew through 2017 and peaked in November 2017 at about R$ 0.99M across 7,289 orders. This may relate to Black Friday, but the data alone does not confirm it.
  • Through 2018, monthly revenue stayed between roughly R$ 0.83M and R$ 0.98M, so growth levelled off.

Quick-Commerce Dark-Store and Delivery Bottleneck Analysis

I built a simulated dataset of 25,000 orders across 5 dark stores to find why evening deliveries run slow, why orders get cancelled at peak hours, and where stockouts cost revenue.

What I did

  • Generated and cleaned the data in Python, flagging missing order values, missing customer IDs and glitch deliveries (0 or extreme minutes) instead of averaging over them.
  • Used SQL, including a window function, to rank hours and check store-level cancellations and delays at peak.
  • Built an Excel what-if model comparing rider hiring cost with recovered orders and revenue.
  • Built a Power BI dashboard of hourly delivery spikes and store-wise cancellation rates.

Findings

  • The all-day average hides the problem. Delivery time jumps to about 21 minutes between 19:00 and 22:00.
  • The stores with the fewest riders, DS05 and DS02, cancel the most orders at peak, with peak deliveries averaging about 24.7-24.9 minutes.
  • Dairy had the highest estimated stockout loss in the sample run, followed by Home and Bakery.
  • Adding 9 riders at ₹18,000 a month costs ₹1,62,000 a month, which can exceed the revenue recovered if cancellations fall only modestly. Hiring should be tested alongside stockout and peak-routing fixes.
